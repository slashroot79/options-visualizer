2Who's on the other side? The clearinghouse chain

Every option has a right-holder and an obligation-holder. Click Next step — each click adds a row so you can watch the contract change hands from start to finish. Nobody is chained to a specific person; the OCC sits in the middle.

OCC — the clearinghouse sits between every buyer & seller in every row below and guarantees the trade. It does not set prices — it just clears & settles. Think coat-check, not shopkeeper.
Each row's right side shows the premium (the option's price per share) at open & close. At expiration the premium = the option's intrinsic value — stock − strike for a call (strike − stock for a put) — because time value is 0 in this model.

StageWhat it means (ELI5)What you must do

★Quick glossary (tap to expand)

Call vs Put

Call = the right to buy at the strike (bullish). Put = the right to sell at the strike (bearish).

Buy (long) vs Sell (short / write)

Buy = pay a premium, get a right, risk limited to what you paid. Sell/Write = collect a premium, take on an obligation, risk can be large. "Sell to open" creates the obligation; "sell to close" just exits a long you already own.

ITM / ATM / OTM (moneyness)

ITM (in the money): a call with stock above the strike, or a put with stock below it — has real exercise value. ATM: stock ≈ strike. OTM: no exercise value yet.

Premium, Strike, Expiration, Assignment

Premium = the option's price (× 100 = dollars, since 1 contract = 100 shares). Strike = the agreed price. Expiration = when the contract dies. Assignment = when a seller is forced to fulfill the obligation because a buyer exercised.

Covered vs Naked

Covered = you hold the offsetting stock/cash, so assignment is painless. Naked = you don't — assignment forces you to buy/sell at market, which can be very costly (see the margin simulator).
